# Energetic stability despite 58 years of population declines in North American bird communities

**Daria Lipsky, Lina K. Mühlbauer, Jose W. Valdez, Henrique M. Pereira, Jes Hines**

*Manuscript submitted to Science*

---

## Overview

This repository contains the R code used to process data, fit models, and produce all figures and statistical tables for the manuscript. We use 58 years of standardised breeding bird count data (1966–2023) from the North American Breeding Bird Survey to track how species richness, abundance, community evenness (Gini coefficient), and community-level energy demand have co-changed over time and across dietary functional guilds.

The central finding is that total community energy demand has remained broadly stable despite a ~34% decline in abundance, sustained by a compensatory compositional shift toward fewer, larger-bodied, higher-trophic-level species. The coupling between per-individual energy demand and community abundance approaches, but is shallower than, strict zero-sum energetic predictions.

## Data

### Breeding Bird Survey (BBS)

Download the BBS dataset from the USGS ScienceBase repository:

> Ziolkowski, D., Lutmerding, M., English, W. B., & Hudson, M.-A. R. (2024). *North American Breeding Bird Survey Dataset (1966–2023)*. U.S. Geological Survey. https://doi.org/10.5066/P136CRBV

Place the state-level count files in `data_2025/States/`, then uncomment the three-line block at the top of Section 2 in `01_gini_bbs_clean.R` to combine them into `States_combined_2025.csv`. The remaining files (`Routes.csv`, `SpeciesList.csv`, `Weather.csv`) are included in the BBS download.

### EltonTraits 1.0

Download the bird functional trait database from:

> Wilman, H., et al. (2014). EltonTraits 1.0: Species-level foraging attributes of the world's birds and mammals. *Ecology*, 95, 2027. https://doi.org/10.1890/13-1917.1

Place `BirdFuncDat.csv` in the `Elton1.0/` directory.

## Workflow

Scripts must be run in order. Set your working directory to the project root before running any script.

### Step 1 — Process raw data

```r
source("01_gini_bbs_clean.R")
```

Reads the raw BBS counts and EltonTraits, computes per-route Gini coefficients and energetic demand metrics, and writes the main analysis dataset to `data_2025/RouteMetrics_2025_diet.csv`. This file is required by all subsequent scripts.

Runtime: approximately 10–20 minutes depending on hardware (a for loop over ~141,000 route-year observations).

### Step 2 — Fit mixed-effects models and produce figures

Each figure script is independent and can be run in any order after Step 1. All figures are saved to the `figures/` directory, which is created automatically.

```r
source("figure_1.R")                    # Figure S3 (supplementary)
source("figure_2_temporal_GLMM.R")      # Figure 1 (main manuscript)
source("figure_3_temporal_GLMM_diet.R") # Figure 3 (main manuscript)
source("figure_4_zerosum.R")            # Figure 4 (main manuscript)
```

#### Model caching

`figure_2_temporal_GLMM.R` and `figure_3_temporal_GLMM_diet.R` cache fitted GLMM objects as `.rds` files in the project root to avoid refitting on subsequent runs. **On the first run**, set `REFIT_GLMM <- TRUE` in the relevant script. Once the cache files exist, reset to `REFIT_GLMM <- FALSE`.

Cache files are named by the analysis window and transformation settings (e.g., `glmm_cache_from1966_raw_logtransform.rds`). They are not included in this repository but will be created locally on first run.

## Energetic demand formula

Per-individual energy demand is calculated as:

$$E_\text{ind} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon^z} \times M^{3/4}$$

where $M$ is mean species body mass in grams (from EltonTraits), $z$ is the species' trophic level (a continuous score from 0 = herbivore to 2 = vertebrate feeder, weighted by dietary proportions following McGill 2008), and $\varepsilon = 0.1$ is the assumed trophic energy transfer efficiency (Lindeman 1942). The $M^{3/4}$ term follows Kleiber's metabolic scaling law (Brown et al. 2004). Route-level total energy demand is obtained by summing $E_\text{ind} \times \text{abundance}$ across all species.
